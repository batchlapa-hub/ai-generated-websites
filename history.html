Evolution of the Frame System

From heavy timber sash to ultra-slim aluminum, our trajectory reflects a century-long pursuit of visual clarity and structural efficiency.

Chronological Development

  1. 1924

    Introduction of the Double-Hung Standard

    Heavy timber frames with 3-inch depth dominate the market, prioritizing insulation over view.

  2. 1968

    First Aluminum Thermal Breaks

    Patents filed for polyamide strips separating interior and exterior aluminum skins.

  3. 2005

    Slim Profile Patent Filing

    Initial research into reducing frame depth to 25mm without sacrificing structural rigidity.

  4. Current Era

    The 18mm Standard

    Achievement of ultra-slim profiles with enhanced acoustic and thermal performance metrics.

Technical History of the Profile

The reduction in profile depth has not been achieved at the expense of insulation. Early attempts to slim the frame resulted in a loss of thermal resistance, leading to condensation issues in cold climates.

Our current technology utilizes a multi-chambered thermal break that is wider than the physical thickness of the aluminum itself. This allows us to maintain a 18mm exterior footprint while housing a thermal barrier equivalent to a 40mm timber section. The cross-section diagram below illustrates how this internal complexity masks behind a visually thin plane.

Material innovation has also played a critical role. We transitioned from standard extruded aluminum to high-grade alloys that retain strength at thinner gauges, allowing us to eliminate the bulky reinforcing bars previously seen in window corners.
