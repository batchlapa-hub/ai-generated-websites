Golden Retriever

A medium-to-large gun dog breed originating in Scotland, known for its gentle nature and high intelligence. Originally bred as a versatile retriever for hunting and fieldwork, the breed was developed to possess a soft mouth that could carry game without damage.

General Characteristics

The Golden Retriever is a substantial dog with a balanced, athletic build. Adult males typically stand between 23 and 24 inches at the shoulder and weigh from 65 to 75 pounds, while females are slightly smaller at 21.5 to 22 inches and 55 to 65 pounds. These dogs generally live an average of ten years, with well-cared-for individuals often reaching up to twelve years.

“The Golden Retriever is a gentle giant.”

Personality and Temperament

This breed consistently ranks among the most trainable dogs, displaying an eager-to-please attitude that makes them ideal for first-time owners. They possess a patient demeanor with children and exhibit strong protective instincts without excessive aggression.

Health & Care Essentials

  • Common Health Issues Hip dysplasia and elbow dysplasia are prevalent genetic conditions; cardiac issues such as subaortic stenosis also occur. Regular veterinary screening is recommended to monitor these specific risks.
  • Coat Care Requirements Their dense double coat requires weekly brushing, increasing to daily during seasonal shedding cycles. Professional grooming every six months helps manage the undercoat and maintain skin health.
  • Nutrition Needs Active dogs require a balanced diet rich in protein for muscle maintenance, though portion control is essential to prevent obesity as they mature past one year of age.
