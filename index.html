Overview of the Subject Breed

The subject breed represents a distinct lineage within the canine family, developed through selective breeding for specific functional roles. Historical records indicate that this group emerged from working stock in Northern Europe during the late 19th century. The primary objective was to create an animal capable of retrieving game and swimming across cold waters, resulting in a physical structure adapted for endurance and strength.

Early registries noted a preference for dogs with dense double coats and webbed feet, traits essential for the harsh environmental conditions of their origin region. As the breed matured, enthusiasts focused on standardizing coat texture and head shape to distinguish it from similar retriever types found in other parts of the world.

Historical Milestones and Development

    First Breed Standard Drafted

    The Norwegian Kennel Club releases the first written breed standard, defining physical parameters.

  2. 1904

    International Recognition Granted

    The breed is officially recognized by the FCI (Fédération Cynologique Internationale).

  3. 1958

    American Kennel Club Registration

    The AKC registers the breed under a specific name, noting its distinct origin from other retriever types.
