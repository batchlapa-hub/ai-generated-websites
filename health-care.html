Health & Wellness Hub

Proactive care is essential for maintaining the vitality of this active breed. This guide focuses on preventative measures and early detection strategies to ensure a long, healthy life.

Hip Dysplasia remains the most prevalent orthopedic condition, affecting approximately 8% to 24% of the population depending on the study. Early screening via X-rays can identify predispositions before symptoms like lameness or stiffness appear in adulthood.

Eye conditions such as Progressive Retinal Atrophy (PRA) and Cataracts are also significant concerns. Regular veterinary ophthalmology exams starting at age two allow for timely intervention. Breed club health surveys consistently recommend genetic testing panels to rule out hereditary defects.

Health Statistics by Age Group

  • Puppy Stage (0-1 year) High risk for developmental hip dysplasia; requires joint supplements.
  • Adult Stage (2-7 years) Peak activity period; focus on weight management to reduce joint stress.
  • Senior Stage (8+ years) Monitor for arthritis and cognitive decline; increase vet visit frequency.

Frequently Asked Health Questions

When should I start genetic testing?

Testing for eye and heart conditions is recommended before breeding, but owners can test adults to understand predispositions.

Are Labradors prone to obesity?

Yes, their love of food makes them susceptible. Strict portion control during the first year of life prevents permanent weight gain issues.

What signs indicate hip dysplasia?

Symptoms include a bunny-rabbit hopping gait, reluctance to jump, and swelling in the hind legs.
