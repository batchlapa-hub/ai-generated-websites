The Stone Stories of Condeixa-a-Nova

Our heritage is a living archive, written in the stones of Roman ruins and preserved in the family archives of historic palaces. We invite you to walk these grounds not just as tourists, but as witnesses to centuries of continuity.

Chronology of Conimbriga

  1. 1st Century AD

    Founding of Conimbriga

    Establishment of the Roman settlement, construction of the amphitheater and baths.

  2. 1095 AD

    Conquest by Henry I

    King Henry I of Portugal seizes the region from Moorish control, establishing a royal castle on the site.

  3. 1760s

    Baroque Renaissance

    Renovation of local noble estates introduces elaborate azulejo tilework and Rococo interiors.

  4. 1922

    Archaeological Park Designation

    The ruins are officially declared a national monument, initiating systematic conservation efforts.

Heritage in Detail

  • Roman Amphitheater Foundation

    Excavated foundations showing the original seating capacity of 10,000 spectators.

  • Church of São Pedro Ruins

    The medieval nave floor plan preserved beneath modern paving stones in the village center.

Why Conimbriga Matters Today

Conimbriga serves as a vital link between Portugal's Roman past and its modern European identity. The site demonstrates how ancient engineering solutions, such as the sophisticated aqueduct system supplying the baths, influenced later architectural developments in the region. Recent studies suggest that the layout of our current town center follows a direct alignment with the original Roman forum grid, proving that urban planning here respects historical geography rather than ignoring it.
